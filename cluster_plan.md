## I. Shared Framing: What "Spire of Excellence" Means

### The Pitch

A spire of excellence is not four faculty doing parallel work. It is an **integrated research infrastructure** where each scholar's questions become sharper because of the others' methods. The four pillars named in the posting — Environmental Economics, Social and Cultural Sustainability, Sustainable Design, and Environmental Planning — only produce a spire when they share data, students, grant proposals, and a common research site.

### Our Thesis

**Texas is a natural laboratory for sustainability science.** The state concentrates every major sustainability challenge in one geography: catastrophic flood risk (Harvey, Tax Day Flood), extreme heat and drought, aging water infrastructure, petrochemical fenceline communities, rapid urbanization, EV transition in a car-dependent landscape, rural broadband deserts, and conservation pressures on agricultural and rangeland. The DFW Metroplex alone spans urban, suburban, exurban, and rural conditions within a one-hour drive from campus.

**Our cluster proposes to build the Texas Sustainability Research Collaborative (TSRC)** — an integrated, place-based research program that makes Texas communities the focal point where environmental economics, environmental justice, spatial planning, and infrastructure policy converge.

## II. Binding Layer + Early Pilot Projects

### The sequencing shift

We move the infrastructure layer up front to show that the cluster is a shared system, not just four parallel research agendas.

### Core 1: Data Core (Lead: Andrews)

**Mission:** Build and govern a shared Texas-focused research infrastructure.

- Texas parcel and property records, tax history, housing market data
- GIS base layers (flood zones, zoning, infrastructure, environmental hazard maps)
- Community and partner-shared qualitative datasets
- Reproducible pipelines for cleaning, versioning, and permissions

**Membership rule:** All faculty are core members, and students participate in at least one project per semester.

### Core 2: Community Engagement Core (Lead: Bray)

**Mission:** Anchor the research in Texas communities and produce research outputs that are interpretable outside academia.

- Maintain community/government/NGO relationships
- Co-design with partners and support culturally appropriate methods
- Translate findings into briefings, workshops, and practitioner-facing reports
- Build a reflexive evaluation framework for continuous learning

**Membership rule:** All faculty actively participate; all students rotate through engagement activity in Year 1.

### Core 3: Student Training Core (Lead: Marable)

**Mission:** Create the cross-college doctoral pathway and a shared student workload model.

- Sustainability Methods Seminar + Dissertation Studio
- Shared summer intensive and RA rotations
- Co-advising, conference mentoring, and publication prep
- Shared graduate assistantship opportunities tied to pilot projects

**Membership rule:** At least one core activity and one faculty mentor per student every term.

### Core 4: Administrative Core (Year 2–3, optional)

**Purpose:** Keep the portfolio coherent as it scales.

- Shared meeting rhythm, grant calendar, and internal reporting
- Workload and leadership rotation
- Cross-unit coordination (departments, UTA offices, partners)

### Early Research Portfolio: Two Pilot Projects (Years 1–3)

To keep Year 1 realistic, we begin with **two pilot projects** with two co-leads each, while retaining all four thematic pillars across the portfolio.

#### Pilot Project A: Equitable Environmental Infrastructure and Community Resilience
**Co-leads:** Bray + Marable

**Core question:** How can water, mobility, and hazard-management systems support resilience without reproducing inequity?

**Pillars covered:**
- Social and Cultural Sustainability
- Environmental Economics
- Environmental Planning

**Core contributions:**
- Data Core: water quality event data, mobility/disruption patterns, infrastructure maps
- Community Engagement Core: partner advisory groups and policy translation
- Student Training Core: student teams produce a community-facing policy memo + one empirical assignment

#### Pilot Project B: Land Use, Conservation, and Climate-Resilient Development
**Co-leads:** Andrews + Shost

**Core question:** Which combinations of conservation finance, design, and planning policy can reduce climate risk while improving equity and affordability?

**Pillars covered:**
- Environmental Economics
- Sustainable Design
- Environmental Planning

**Core contributions:**
- Data Core: parcel datasets, easement and land-use data, floodplain exposure maps
- Community Engagement Core: community design workshops and outcome briefings
- Student Training Core: one RA-backed pilot methods component (valuation, spatial analysis, or policy simulation)

**Why two pilots first?**

- Each can pursue **pilot pathways** (internal/state/regional) with manageable scope.
- Each can scale into a larger project if early findings are promising.
- After 3–5 years, the two pilots feed a joint center-style proposal if they demonstrate shared scientific and community value.

## IV. PhD Pipeline Design

### Structure: The Sustainability Doctoral Fellows Program

The cluster proposes a cross-college doctoral fellows program that trains the next generation of sustainability scholars who are comfortable working across disciplinary boundaries.

#### Cohort Model

- **Target:** 2 PhD students per cluster faculty member = **8 doctoral students** across the cluster at steady state (Years 3–5)
- **Ramp-up:** 1 student each in Year 1 (4 total), second student each in Year 2 (8 total)
- **Funding:** Each student supported by a combination of:
  - RA lines on cluster grants (primary — budgeted into every proposal)
  - UTA doctoral stipends (6 years of tuition/fee coverage + stipend for History PhD students; comparable packages in Economics and Sociology)
  - Summer research assistantships on cluster grants
  - Travel funds for conference presentations

#### Where Students Are Housed

| Faculty | Doctoral Program | College |
|---------|-----------------|---------|
| Andrews | PhD in History (Transatlantic) — Geography/Environmental Planning track | Liberal Arts |
| Bray | PhD in Sociology, or interdisciplinary program | Liberal Arts or Social Work |
| Shost | PhD in Economics or Business Administration | Business |
| Marable | PhD in Economics or Business Administration | Business |

**Note:** Students are admitted to their home program but participate in the cluster's shared activities. This cross-college model is exactly what RISE 100 is designed to create.

#### Joint Training Components

**1. Sustainability Methods Seminar (Annual, Fall semester)**

A rotating seminar where each cluster faculty member teaches a 3–4 week module on their core methods. All cluster PhD students attend regardless of home department.

| Module | Faculty | Content | Weeks |
|--------|---------|---------|-------|
| Spatial Analysis for Sustainability | Andrews | GIS, Geo-AI, spatial statistics, digital mapping | 3–4 |
| Community-Engaged Research Methods | Bray | Participatory action research, qualitative methods, ethical community partnerships | 3–4 |
| Causal Identification in Environmental Economics | Shost or Marable | Quasi-experimental design, IV strategies, large dataset methods | 3–4 |
| Environmental Policy Analysis | Marable or Shost | Cost-benefit analysis, welfare economics, revealed preference | 3–4 |

This seminar exposes every student to all four methodological traditions. An economics student learns to read a GIS map; a geography student learns causal identification logic; a sociology student learns spatial statistics. **This is the cross-training that makes cluster graduates distinctive on the job market.**

**2. Dissertation Studio (Biweekly, Spring semester)**

A workshop format where students present dissertation work-in-progress to the full cluster (all faculty + all students). Modeled on architecture and design studio critique:
- Each student presents dissertation work at regular intervals in Dissertation Studio
- Feedback from faculty and students across all four disciplines
- Focus on how each dissertation connects to the pilot projects and cluster cores
- Students learn to communicate across disciplinary boundaries

**3. Summer Research Intensives**

Each summer, cluster students participate in a 2-week intensive fieldwork or data collection period in the shared Texas study region. Activities include:
- Joint site visits to study communities (flood zones, fenceline neighborhoods, conservation areas, water systems)
- Collaborative data collection that feeds both pilot projects
- Cross-training workshops (e.g., economics students learn GIS field methods; geography students learn survey design)

**4. Conference Pipeline**

Each cluster student presents at least once per year at a national conference. The cluster coordinates submissions so that students appear on shared panels:
- AAG (American Association of Geographers) — Andrews' students
- ASA/RSS (American Sociological Association / Rural Sociological Society) — Bray's students
- AERE / SEA (Association of Environmental and Resource Economists / Southern Economic Association) — Shost and Marable's students
- Cluster-organized panels at interdisciplinary venues (AAAS, Sustainability Science conferences)

#### Mentoring Model

**Primary advisor:** Each student's home faculty member
**Secondary advisor:** A second cluster faculty member from a different discipline

| Student Home | Primary | Secondary | Benefit |
|-------------|---------|-----------|---------|
| Geography (Andrews) | Andrews | Bray or Marable | Adds qualitative or economic lens to spatial work |
| Sociology (Bray) | Bray | Andrews or Shost | Adds spatial or economic methods to justice research |
| Economics (Shost) | Shost | Andrews or Bray | Adds spatial or community engagement to econometrics |
| Economics (Marable) | Marable | Andrews or Bray | Adds spatial or justice framing to infrastructure research |

This **cross-advising model** ensures every dissertation has an interdisciplinary committee member from within the cluster, and every student has a mentor who can push them beyond their home discipline.

#### Student Deliverables by Year

| Year | Milestone |
|------|-----------|
| Year 1 | Complete coursework + Sustainability Methods Seminar; begin RA work on cluster grants; identify dissertation topic area |
| Year 2 | Present at Dissertation Studio; attend Summer Research Intensive; present at 1 national conference; draft prospectus |
| Year 3 | Defend prospectus; submit first co-authored paper with advisor; collect primary dissertation data |
| Year 4 | Present at 2+ conferences; submit sole-authored or lead-authored paper; draft dissertation chapters |
| Year 5 | Complete dissertation; go on job market with portfolio of 2+ publications, conference presentations, and cross-disciplinary training record |
