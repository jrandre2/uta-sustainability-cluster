# Day-1 Collaborative Deck Outline (Expanded)

_16-slide outline for the March 26 cluster presentation_
_Prepared March 19, 2026_

## Deck Goal

Present a collaborative, fundable, student-centered vision for how the cluster would help UTA build a "spire of excellence" across environmental economics, social and cultural sustainability, sustainable design, and environmental planning.

This expanded version keeps the original presentation spine, but gives much more visible space to:

- named funding sources
- funding ownership by faculty
- a semester-by-semester proposal timeline
- the relationship between research themes and fundable work

## Slide 1: Spire of Excellence at UTA

**Purpose:** answer the prompt immediately with one clear thesis.

**Core message:**

- a spire of excellence is shared infrastructure, shared students, shared grants, and shared public impact
- this cluster should operate as one coordinated research program, not four parallel hires

**Must include:**

- the four pillars named in the prompt
- the phrase "Texas as a natural laboratory" or equivalent
- one line defining what success looks like at UTA

**Visual idea:**

- four-pillar diagram converging into one cluster

## Slide 2: Why Texas, Why UTA, Why This Cluster

**Purpose:** make the place-based argument concrete.

**Core message:**

- Texas concentrates flood risk, drought, energy transition, land-use pressure, environmental justice, and infrastructure stress in one geography
- UTA is well-positioned to study these problems through cross-college collaboration and regional partnerships

**Must include:**

- DFW/Texas examples: flood risk, EV buildout, water systems, fast urban growth
- one sentence on UTA's institutional and geographic advantage

**Visual idea:**

- Texas or DFW map with challenge icons

## Slide 3: How the Cluster Will Work as One System

**Purpose:** show the operating model before listing projects.

**Core message:**

- the cluster will be held together by shared operating cores rather than informal collaboration

**Must include:**

- data core
- community engagement core
- student training core
- governance / coordination layer

**Visual idea:**

- hub-and-spoke or layered system diagram

## Slide 4: Pilot A: Equitable Environmental Infrastructure

**Purpose:** show one flagship collaborative project.

**Core message:**

- the first pilot asks how water, mobility, and hazard-management systems can improve resilience without reproducing inequity

**Must include:**

- Bray + Marable co-lead framing
- partner examples: Arlington, TRWD, NCTCOG, TDEM
- likely outputs: policy memos, community-facing briefs, empirical student projects

**Funding tie-ins to name on the slide:**

- NSF DRMS
- NOAA BAA
- NCTCOG NTCMT
- TWDB or TCEQ, depending on final emphasis

**Visual idea:**

- infrastructure -> resilience/equity question -> student and policy outputs

## Slide 5: Pilot B: Land Use, Conservation, Resilient Development

**Purpose:** show the second collaborative project and economic/planning complementarity.

**Core message:**

- the second pilot asks how conservation finance, planning, and design policy can reduce climate risk while improving affordability and equity

**Must include:**

- Andrews + Shost co-lead framing
- parcel, land-use, floodplain, conservation, and affordability data
- one clear design/planning/economic tradeoff question

**Funding tie-ins to name on the slide:**

- USDA AFRI
- NSF Environmental Sustainability
- Meadows Foundation
- GLO Coastal Management Program

**Visual idea:**

- land-use / conservation / affordability tradeoff diagram

## Slide 6: Doctoral Pipeline and Cross-Training

**Purpose:** answer the PhD support part of the prompt directly.

**Core message:**

- the cluster will produce doctoral students who are trained in multiple methods and can work across disciplinary boundaries

**Must include:**

- Year 1 target: 4 students
- steady-state target: 8 students
- primary advisor + secondary cluster advisor model
- sustainability methods seminar
- dissertation studio

**Funding logic to mention:**

- RA support built into all major grant proposals
- doctoral support tied to pilot projects, summer intensives, and conference funding

**Visual idea:**

- recruitment -> methods seminar -> dissertation studio -> placement pipeline

## Slide 7: Pedagogy and Student Integration

**Purpose:** show how research becomes teaching and public-facing student work.

**Core message:**

- the cluster connects undergraduate learning, graduate training, and applied sustainability research

**Must include:**

- undergraduate pathway or practicum/capstone
- community-engaged or project-based learning
- research-to-teaching integration

**Avoid:**

- long course catalogs
- overly detailed curriculum lists

**Visual idea:**

- undergrad -> practicum -> graduate training -> research pipeline

## Slide 8: Funding Strategy: From Seed to Scale

**Purpose:** introduce the full funding architecture before naming specific programs.

**Core message:**

- the cluster will use a three-stage funding strategy: Seed and Pilot, Scale, and Flagship Growth

**Must include:**

- Phase 1: Year 1 seed/pilot funding
- Phase 2: Years 1-3 scale and convergence funding
- Phase 3: Years 3-5 sustained growth and faculty development
- total 5-year pipeline estimate: **$7.5M-$15M**

**Visual idea:**

- ladder or funnel labeled Seed -> Scale -> Flagship

## Slide 9: Year 1 Federal Funding Sources

**Purpose:** show that the cluster has credible immediate federal targets.

**Core message:**

- Year 1 is not speculative; there are confirmed or plausible federal programs that align directly with the pilots

**Must include:**

- NSF DRMS: first cluster proposal, disaster risk and resilience
- NOAA BAA: flood and climate resilience
- NSF Environmental Sustainability: PI-level water/conservation work
- USDA AFRI: rural resilience, conservation, and water quality
- NEH Collaborative Research: historical geography / environmental justice, if the humanities angle is emphasized

**Recommended format:**

- 4-5 grant cards with agency, thematic fit, and likely lead

**Speaking point:**

- emphasize that these are not random grants; they map directly onto the cluster's first two pilots

## Slide 10: Texas, Regional, and Foundation Funding Sources

**Purpose:** show that the cluster understands Texas's funding ecosystem, not just federal programs.

**Core message:**

- state, regional, foundation, and internal funding make the cluster more competitive and let UTA move quickly

**Must include:**

- Meadows Foundation: shared data infrastructure and community partnerships
- NCTCOG NTCMT: EV and transportation research, with UTA already a partner
- GLO Coastal Management Program: applied resilience opportunity
- UTA IRP: internal seed support
- TWDB, TxDOT, or TCEQ as the Texas-agency layer

**Recommended format:**

- one-column "Texas advantage" slide with short why-it-matters notes

**Speaking point:**

- these sources make federal proposals stronger by funding data, partnerships, and pilot evidence first

## Slide 11: Scale and Flagship Funding Sources (Years 2-3)

**Purpose:** show the medium-term grants that turn pilots into a visible cluster program.

**Core message:**

- Year 1 funding creates pilot data and partnerships; Years 2-3 build toward major interdisciplinary grants

**Must include:**

- NSF S&CC: smart community resilience and Geo-AI
- NSF GCR: flagship interdisciplinary convergence proposal
- CAREER pathways for eligible faculty as part of cluster growth
- monitored opportunities: Kresge, RWJF, FEMA BRIC if viable, DOE where appropriate

**Recommended format:**

- scale pathway with "pilot data -> convergence proposal -> long-term portfolio"

**Speaking point:**

- frame GCR as the aspiration grant that the earlier proposals are designed to make competitive

## Slide 12: Funding Ownership Map by Faculty

**Purpose:** prove the cluster has role clarity instead of vague collective ambition.

**Core message:**

- each faculty member owns part of the funding map, while the cluster shares the flagship opportunities

**Must include:**

- Andrews: NSF DRMS, NOAA, NSF S&CC, NEH, TWDB/GLO
- Bray: NEH, RWJF, Kresge, TCEQ-type community/environmental justice pathways
- Shost: USDA AFRI, NSF Environmental Sustainability, CAREER-eligible pathways
- Marable: NSF DRMS/CBET-type work, DOE-style infrastructure pathways, NCTCOG, TxDOT
- all four: NSF GCR and shared cluster proposals

**Visual idea:**

- four-column matrix: faculty -> agencies -> pilot/project role

## Slide 13: 18-Month Proposal Timeline

**Purpose:** show the order in which the funding work unfolds.

**Core message:**

- the cluster will not pursue everything at once; it will sequence proposals to build credibility and scale

**Must include:**

- late Summer/Fall 2026: DRMS, NOAA, Meadows, NCTCOG, UTA IRP
- late 2026 / early 2027: AFRI, NEH, pilot-data buildout
- 2027 cycle: S&CC, GCR preparation/submission path, TWDB/TxDOT/TCEQ where appropriate

**Recommended format:**

- semester-based proposal timeline with 5-7 named opportunities

**Speaking point:**

- exact solicitation calendars should be rechecked, but the strategic order should be visible

## Slide 14: 18-Month Rollout: Fall 2026 Quick Wins

**Purpose:** show what the cluster can do immediately upon arrival.

**Core message:**

- the first semester should show motion in grants, partnerships, student recruitment, and shared infrastructure

**Must include:**

- launch biweekly cluster meetings
- stand up shared data infrastructure
- partner outreach to Arlington, TRWD, NCTCOG, and UTA centers
- submit first round of seed/pilot proposals
- recruit the first doctoral students

**Visual idea:**

- Fall 2026 checklist or milestone bar

## Slide 15: 18-Month Rollout: Spring-Fall 2027 Milestones

**Purpose:** show how the cluster becomes visible and durable.

**Core message:**

- the second and third semesters turn the cluster from an idea into a funded, student-producing, externally visible program

**Must include:**

- launch sustainability methods seminar
- begin fieldwork / pilot data collection
- host Dissertation Studio
- prepare or submit the flagship interdisciplinary proposal
- produce first co-authored paper and first shared conference panel

**Visual idea:**

- Spring 2027 -> Fall 2027 milestone timeline

## Slide 16: Governance, Metrics, and Closing Value Proposition

**Purpose:** end with accountability and institutional value.

**Core message:**

- the cluster will sustain collaboration through simple governance, shared accountability, and measurable outcomes

**Must include:**

- biweekly meetings
- rotating coordination
- shared workspace and reporting rhythm
- success metrics: proposals, funded projects, students, partnerships, publications, public visibility
- closing sentence on why this makes UTA distinctive in sustainability research

**Visual idea:**

- compact metrics dashboard with a one-line closing claim

## Notes for the Room

- Keep Slides 1-7 close to the current 10-slide structure so the presentation still feels coherent.
- Use Slides 8-13 to demonstrate that the cluster has a real funding plan, not just broad ambition.
- Use Slides 14-15 to show sequence and execution discipline.
- If time gets tight, merge Slides 9 and 10, and merge Slides 14 and 15.
- Keep text minimal and use grant names selectively. The point is not to dump a spreadsheet on the screen; it is to show that the cluster understands sequence, ownership, and fit.
