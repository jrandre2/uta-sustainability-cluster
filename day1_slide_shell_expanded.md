## Slide 1: Spire of Excellence at UTA

**Lead:** Andrews  
**Target time:** 1.0 min

### Draft slide text

**Title:** Building a Spire of Excellence in Sustainability at UTA

**Subtitle:** A collaborative cluster model for research, doctoral mentorship, and external funding

**On-slide bullets:**

- Not four parallel hires, but one coordinated sustainability research system
- Shared infrastructure, shared students, shared grants, shared public impact
- Texas as a natural laboratory for interdisciplinary sustainability research

**Visual:**

- four pillars converging upward into a single spire-like cluster form:
  - Environmental Economics
  - Social and Cultural Sustainability
  - Sustainable Design
  - Environmental Planning

### Speaker notes

- Open by answering the prompt directly: the cluster builds excellence by functioning as an integrated system.
- Define "spire of excellence" as a structure that produces grants, students, partnerships, and visible public-facing work.
- Set the place-based frame immediately: Texas gives the cluster a coherent shared research terrain.
- If the room is tense or fragmented, keep the opener especially simple: "our proposal is a shared research system with common projects, common students, and a common funding pipeline."
- Do not spend time on candidate biographies here. The purpose of this slide is alignment, not introduction.
- Let the visual do part of the rhetorical work: the four pillars should visibly rise into a common apex rather than sit as four equal disconnected boxes.
- Handoff: "That logic starts with why Texas and why UTA are unusually strong for this work."

## Slide 8: Funding Strategy: From Seed to Scale

**Target time:** 1.0 min

**Title:** Funding Strategy: Seed, Scale, Flagship

**On-slide bullets:**

- **Phase 1:** Seed and pilot funding in Year 1
- **Phase 2:** Scale and convergence funding in Years 1-3

  - Scale and Convergence

## Slide 9: Year 1 Federal Funding Sources

**Lead:** Andrews + Marable + Shost  
**Target time:** 1.5 min

### Draft slide text

**Title:** Year 1 Federal Funding Sources

**On-slide bullets:**

- **NSF DRMS**: first cluster proposal on disaster risk and resilience
- **NOAA BAA**: flood and climate resilience in Texas communities
- **NSF Environmental Sustainability**: water, conservation, and applied sustainability research
- **USDA AFRI**: rural resilience, natural resource systems, and conservation economics
- **NEH Collaborative Research**: environmental justice and historical geography, where relevant

**Suggested note boxes or side labels:**

- Near-term submission targets
- Direct fit with Pilot A and Pilot B
- Clear faculty leads already identifiable

**Visual:**

- five grant cards arranged on a slight rise beneath a subtle spire cap, with agency, thematic fit, and likely lead

### Speaker notes

- Andrews should lead with DRMS as the clearest first collective move.
- Marable can speak to DRMS/NOAA and infrastructure-oriented fit.
- Shost can speak to AFRI and Environmental Sustainability.
- Mention NEH only if the group wants to preserve a humanities-facing angle; otherwise keep it secondary.
- The key line: these are not random grants. They directly map to the two pilot projects.
- Use the following quick-reference details if needed:
  - **NSF DRMS:** confirmed active; roughly $100K-$400K; August 18, 2026 deadline; best first cluster submission because it supports disaster risk, resilience, and post-disaster data collection.
  - **NOAA BAA:** confirmed active; September 30, 2026; flexible amount; strong for flood and climate resilience work in Texas coastal and inland systems.
  - **NSF Environmental Sustainability:** roughly $300K; rolling; best for individual or smaller-team proposals around water quality, conservation, and sustainability systems.
  - **USDA AFRI Foundational:** up to $10M depending on track; December 31, 2026; strongest federal fit for conservation economics, rural resilience, and water-quality work.
  - **NEH Collaborative Research:** up to $250K; likely next cycle in fall 2026; useful only if the group wants to foreground historical geography and environmental justice as part of the cluster identity.
- Suggested one-line framing: "In Year 1 we would lead with DRMS, then layer AFRI, NOAA, and Environmental Sustainability around the two pilot projects."
- If time is tight, name only DRMS, AFRI, and NOAA.
- Avoid mentioning HEGS-TREES on the main slide unless asked; the fit is strong, but the solicitation status is uncertain.
- If asked about HEGS-TREES: "It is one of the best intellectual fits, but NSF currently lists it as waiting for a new publication, so we would monitor it rather than build the public presentation around it."
- The card arrangement should suggest progression, not a flat grant menu.
- Handoff: "Federal funding is only part of the story. Texas-specific funding makes the cluster faster and more competitive."

**Title:** Texas, Regional, and Foundation Funding Sources

**On-slide bullets:**

- **Meadows Foundation**: shared data infrastructure and community partnerships

- **GLO Coastal Management Program**: applied resilience and coastal planning

- **TWDB / TxDOT / TCEQ**: Texas agency pathways for flood, mobility, and water-quality research

### Speaker notes

- Stress that state and local sources are not side notes; they are part of the cluster's competitive strategy.
- Meadows is the cleanest foundation example because it supports flexible infrastructure and partnership building.
- NCTCOG matters because it gives the cluster a regional vehicle with an existing UTA connection.
- Use one sentence to connect these sources to credibility: Texas-specific seed funding strengthens later federal submissions.
- Use the following quick-reference details if needed:
  - **Meadows Foundation:** active and rolling; Texas universities are eligible; documented grants range from about $500K to multi-million-dollar awards; strongest philanthropic fit for shared infrastructure, community partnership building, and capacity development.
  - **NCTCOG NTCMT:** strong regional mobility pathway with an existing UTA connection; confirm current matching terms and submission cycle directly with NCTCOG before quoting specifics; best fit for EV, transportation, and mobility-related work.
  - **GLO Coastal Management Program:** active with a June 3, 2026 deadline; useful as an example of an immediate Texas state resilience opportunity.
  - **UTA IRP:** roughly $20K internal seed funding; next likely cycle around November 2026; valuable because it explicitly expects a follow-on external proposal.
  - **TWDB / TxDOT / TCEQ:** these are the agencies that make the "Texas story" concrete by supporting flood, transportation, and water-quality research.

- If asked which non-federal source should be first, answer Meadows.
- If asked which is the easiest near-term regional opening, answer NCTCOG.
- If asked which Texas agencies best reinforce Pilot A, answer TWDB and TCEQ; for Pilot B, answer GLO and TxDOT depending on emphasis.

- **Monitor and align**: Kresge, RWJF, FEMA BRIC, DOE where program conditions fit

**Visual:**

- Position GCR as the aspiration grant the cluster is building toward, not as a first-semester fantasy.
- Explain that the medium-term portfolio depends on Year 1 data, partner commitments, and visible pilot outputs.
