## Tier 1: Confirmed Active — Submit Within 6 Months

### NSF Decision, Risk and Management Sciences (DRMS)

- **Status:** CONFIRMED ACTIVE (rolling)
- **Deadlines:** August 18, 2026 and January 19, 2027 (target dates)
- **Funding:** Typically $100K–$400K per standard grant; also supports RAPID grants for ephemeral disaster data
- **URL:** nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/decision-risk-management-sciences-drms

**Why this is our best first grant:**
DRMS has the nearest confirmed deadline and fits the cluster perfectly. It covers disaster risk decision-making, flood risk perception, community-level hazard mitigation decisions, conservation easement adoption, and consumer response to environmental violations. It supports both behavioral and computational approaches.

**Role map:**
- Andrews (PI): Flood risk and housing decision modeling
- Marable (co-PI): Consumer response to water quality violations
- Bray (co-PI): Community risk perception and participatory risk communication
- Shost (co-PI): Conservation land-use decision-making

**Note:** DRMS also supports RAPID grants ($50K–$200K, fast-tracked) for time-sensitive post-disaster research — relevant for Texas flood events.

### USDA AFRI Foundational and Applied Science

- **Status:** CONFIRMED ACTIVE
- **Deadline:** December 31, 2026
- **Funding:** $300M total pool; awards $10K–$10M
- **URL:** nifa.usda.gov/grants/funding-opportunities

**Cluster fit:** Covers six priority areas including "Bioenergy, Natural Resources, and Environment" and "Agriculture Economics and Rural Communities." Supports water quality, environmental economics, natural resources, community vitality, and rural development research. UTA is eligible as a state institution.

**Role map:**
- Shost (PI): Conservation/land economics, agricultural land use
- Marable (co-PI): Water quality and economic impacts on agricultural communities
- Andrews (co-PI): Spatial analysis of rural environmental systems, broadband/rural infrastructure
- Bray (co-PI): Rural sociology, community vitality, participatory research

### NSF Environmental Sustainability (CBET)

- **Status:** CONFIRMED ACTIVE (full proposals accepted anytime, no set deadline)
- **Funding:** Typically supports one graduate student per award; 3-year standard or 5-year CAREER ($400K–$500K minimum over 5 years)
- **URL:** nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/environmental-sustainability

**Cluster fit:** Covers circular bioeconomy, industrial ecology, green engineering, ecological engineering, and earth systems engineering including climate adaptation. Good for individual PI proposals from Marable (water quality/sustainability economics), Shost (conservation/land economics), or Andrews (spatial modeling of environmental systems).

## Tier 2: Build Toward — 6–12 Month Horizon (Confirmed Active)

### NSF Growing Convergence Research (GCR)

- **Status:** CONFIRMED ACTIVE
- **Deadline:** February 8, 2027
- **Funding:** Phase I up to $1.2M (years 1–2), Phase II up to $2.4M (years 3–5); 6–10 awards
- **URL:** nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/growing-convergence-research-gcr

**Why this is our flagship long-term grant:**
GCR is NSF's premier program for research that "transcends disciplinary boundaries with novel conceptual frameworks." It is specifically designed for clusters like ours. A $2.4M project on "Equitable Environmental Resilience: Integrating Economic, Social, and Spatial Dimensions of Sustainability in Texas" would demonstrate exactly the convergence that RISE 100 is building.

**Role map:**
- Andrews (PI): Spatial/computational methods, Geo-AI, project integration
- Bray (co-PI): Environmental justice theory, community engagement, qualitative methods
- Shost (co-PI): Conservation economics, market-based policy mechanisms
- Marable (co-PI): Environmental infrastructure economics, consumer welfare analysis

**Strategy:** Begin with a DRMS or AFRI pilot in Year 1, then use the results to build the GCR Phase I application by February 2027. If HEGS-TREES reopens, that becomes an additional pilot vehicle.

### NSF Smart and Connected Communities (S&CC)
_NSF 25-527_

- **Status:** CONFIRMED ACTIVE
- **Next deadline:** Preliminary proposal due September 14, 2026; full proposal target date November 9, 2026 (recurring second-Monday cycle)
- **Funding:** Planning Grants up to $150K (1 yr); Integrative Research Grants (IRG) up to $1.5M (4 yr)
- **URL:** nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/smart-and-connected-communities-scc

**Cluster fit:** S&CC funds projects that integrate intelligent systems with community needs. Requires community stakeholder engagement and a specific community partner. A project on smart flood monitoring, rural broadband-enabled resilience, or EV charging network optimization in underserved Texas communities.

**Role map:**
- Andrews (PI): Geo-AI/smart systems, spatial data infrastructure
- Marable (co-PI): EV infrastructure/consumer behavior
- Bray (co-PI): Community engagement, environmental justice
- Shost (co-PI): Market-based policy analysis

**Note:** NSF 25-543 (Future Computing Research) is a separate program focused on computing infrastructure, not community sustainability. The correct program for this cluster is S&CC (NSF 25-527).

_NSF 25-507_

- **Deadline:** Previously August 1 (recurring annually); **TBD for 2026**

- **URL:** nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/human-environment-geographical-sciences-hegs

**Why this would be our best first grant (if the solicitation reopens):**
The TREES track is purpose-built for a cluster like ours. It rewards integration of spatial analysis (Andrews), environmental social science (Bray), environmental economics (Shost, Marable), and place-based research. A proposal on "Spatial Dimensions of Environmental Risk and Resilience in Texas Communities" would draw on all four candidates' strengths.

**Action:** Monitor NSF for the updated solicitation. If it reopens, treat as top priority. In the meantime, lead with DRMS (confirmed active, Aug 2026 deadline) as the first cluster proposal.

**Role map:**
- Andrews (PI): GIS/spatial analysis framework, flood risk modeling, data infrastructure
- Bray (co-PI): Community engagement, environmental justice dimensions, qualitative data
- Shost (co-PI): Conservation economics, land use policy analysis
- Marable (co-PI): Water infrastructure economics, consumer behavior modeling

- **NSF GCR** (Phase I up to $1.2M; Phase II up to $2.4M) — "Equitable Environmental Resilience" — our flagship interdisciplinary proposal, built on Year 1 pilot data, February 2027 deadline

### Flagship (Year 2–3): Convergence
- **NSF GCR Phase II** ($2.4M) — scale the convergence program with Year 1–2 pilot data

## Strategic Notes

1. **Lead with DRMS in the presentation.** It has the nearest confirmed deadline (Aug 18, 2026), fits all four candidates, and demonstrates a concrete first step. Mention HEGS-TREES as a strong fit "pending solicitation reopening" — this shows awareness of the funding landscape without overpromising.

2. **GCR is the aspiration grant.** Frame it as the 2–3 year goal that everything else builds toward. The committee wants to see a trajectory, not just a list. GCR is confirmed active with a Feb 2027 deadline.

3. **Assign grant leads by expertise.** In the presentation, each candidate should "own" specific funding streams:
   - Andrews: NSF DRMS, NSF S&CC, NOAA, NSF HEGS (if reopened)
   - Bray: NEH, RWJF, Kresge CCHE/CREWS
   - Shost: USDA AFRI, NSF Environmental Sustainability
   - Marable: NSF DRMS, NSF CBET, Kresge EJET, DOE (when VTO reopens)
   - All four: NSF GCR (flagship)

4. **Texas-specific angles to emphasize:**
   - DFW Metroplex flood risk (Trinity River watershed, urban flash flooding)
   - Texas Gulf Coast hurricane vulnerability (Harvey-type events)
   - Rural West Texas broadband gaps and climate adaptation
   - Texas petrochemical fenceline communities (environmental justice)
   - Texas EV infrastructure buildout in sprawling metro areas
   - Texas water supply challenges (drought, groundwater depletion, aging infrastructure)

5. **UTA's existing infrastructure to reference:**
   - CTEDD (USDOT Tier-1 Transportation Center) — natural partner for Marable's EV work
   - CARIDA (Center for AI and Big Data) — natural partner for Andrews' Geo-AI work
   - TACC access + UTA's new $2.1M supercomputing hub — computational infrastructure
   - Center for Greater Southwestern Studies — digital humanities and GIS projects
   - Day Family Research Lab — data visualization and analysis
