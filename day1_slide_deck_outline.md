# Day-1 Collaborative Deck Outline

_10-slide outline for the March 26 cluster presentation_
_Prepared March 19, 2026_

## Deck Goal

Present a collaborative, fundable, student-centered vision for how the cluster would help UTA build a "spire of excellence" across environmental economics, social and cultural sustainability, sustainable design, and environmental planning.

## Slide 1: Spire of Excellence at UTA

**Purpose:** answer the prompt immediately with one clear thesis.

**Core message:**

- a spire of excellence is shared infrastructure, shared students, shared grants, and shared public impact
- this cluster should operate as one coordinated research program, not four parallel hires

**Must include:**

- the four pillars named in the prompt
- the phrase "Texas as a natural laboratory" or equivalent
- one line defining what success looks like at UTA

**Visual idea:**

- four-pillar diagram converging into one central cluster

## Slide 2: Why Texas, Why UTA, Why This Cluster

**Purpose:** make the place-based argument concrete.

**Core message:**

- Texas concentrates flood risk, drought, energy transition, land-use pressure, environmental justice, and infrastructure stress in one geography
- UTA is well-positioned to study these problems through cross-college collaboration and regional partnerships

**Must include:**

- DFW/Texas examples: flood risk, EV buildout, water systems, fast urban growth
- one sentence on UTA's location and institutional advantage

**Visual idea:**

- Texas or DFW map with 4-5 challenge icons

## Slide 3: How the Cluster Will Work as One System

**Purpose:** show structure before listing projects.

**Core message:**

- the cluster will be held together by shared operating cores rather than informal collaboration

**Must include:**

- data core
- community engagement core
- student training core
- optional administrative/governance layer

**Visual idea:**

- hub-and-spoke or stacked operating model diagram

## Slide 4: Pilot A: Equitable Environmental Infrastructure

**Purpose:** show one flagship collaborative project.

**Core message:**

- the first pilot asks how water, mobility, and hazard-management systems can improve resilience without reproducing inequity

**Must include:**

- Bray + Marable co-lead framing
- partner examples: Arlington, TRWD, NCTCOG, TDEM
- likely outputs: policy memos, community-facing briefs, empirical student projects

**Visual idea:**

- problem-to-output flow: infrastructure -> inequity/resilience question -> policy outcomes

## Slide 5: Pilot B: Land Use, Conservation, Resilient Development

**Purpose:** show the second collaborative project and complementarity.

**Core message:**

- the second pilot asks how conservation finance, planning, and design policy can reduce climate risk while improving affordability and equity

**Must include:**

- Andrews + Shost co-lead framing
- parcel, land-use, floodplain, conservation, and affordability data
- a clear planning/economic tradeoff question

**Visual idea:**

- land-use / floodplain / development triangle or parcel-data graphic

## Slide 6: Doctoral Pipeline and Cross-Training

**Purpose:** answer the PhD support part of the prompt directly.

**Core message:**

- the cluster will produce doctoral students who are trained in multiple methods and can work across disciplinary boundaries

**Must include:**

- Year 1 target: 4 students
- steady-state target: 8 students
- primary advisor + secondary cluster advisor model
- sustainability methods seminar
- dissertation studio

**Visual idea:**

- student pipeline timeline from recruitment to dissertation to placement

## Slide 7: Pedagogy and Student Integration

**Purpose:** show how research becomes teaching and public-facing student work.

**Core message:**

- the cluster will connect undergraduate learning, graduate training, and applied sustainability research

**Must include:**

- undergraduate pathway or practicum/capstone
- community-engaged or project-based learning
- research-to-teaching integration

**Avoid:**

- long course catalogs
- overly detailed curriculum lists

**Visual idea:**

- undergrad -> practicum -> graduate training -> research pipeline

## Slide 8: Funding Strategy: Year 1 to Year 3

**Purpose:** show that the cluster has a credible funding trajectory.

**Core message:**

- the cluster has immediate seed targets, medium-term scale targets, and one flagship convergence goal

**Must include:**

- NSF DRMS as first concrete move
- NSF GCR as flagship goal
- one additional federal target: USDA AFRI, NOAA, or NSF S&CC
- one foundation/regional target: Meadows Foundation or NCTCOG NTCMT
- one Texas/state angle: TWDB, GLO, TxDOT, or TCEQ

**Visual idea:**

- three-stage funding pipeline: Seed -> Scale -> Flagship

## Slide 9: 18-Month Rollout and Early Wins

**Purpose:** prove the plan is actionable.

**Core message:**

- the cluster can move from hiring to visible activity quickly

**Must include:**

- Fall 2026: first proposals, shared meetings, partner outreach, initial student recruitment
- Spring 2027: methods seminar, fieldwork, pilot data collection
- Fall 2027: GCR build, first co-authored paper, public visibility

**Visual idea:**

- semester-by-semester roadmap

## Slide 10: Governance, Metrics, and Closing Value Proposition

**Purpose:** end with seriousness, accountability, and fit.

**Core message:**

- the cluster will sustain collaboration through simple governance, shared accountability, and visible outcomes

**Must include:**

- biweekly meetings
- rotating coordination
- shared data/workspace
- success metrics: proposals, students, partnerships, outputs
- final sentence on why this makes UTA distinctive in sustainability research

**Visual idea:**

- short metrics dashboard plus one closing sentence

## Notes for the Room

- Keep speaker handoffs short and explicit.
- Do not add standalone researcher bios unless the group insists.
- If time gets tight, merge Slides 2 and 3, and merge Slides 9 and 10.
- Keep the deck visually light: one idea per slide, one graphic per slide, minimal prose.
