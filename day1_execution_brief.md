# Day-1 Execution Brief

_Practical guide for the March 26 collaborative presentation build_
_Prepared March 19, 2026_

## Objective

Use the first 10 minutes of the 2-hour prep session to help the group converge on a clean structure that:

- answers every part of the prompt
- gives each candidate visible ownership
- sounds collaborative rather than pre-scripted
- can survive time compression without falling apart

## Working Goal for the Room

By the 15-minute mark, the group should agree on:

1. a 6-slide presentation structure
2. one shared thesis
3. one owner for each section
4. one person editing the deck in real time
5. one backup cut list if the committee shortens the talk

## Default Thesis

UTA can build a real "spire of excellence" if the cluster functions as a shared research system rather than four parallel hires. The strongest unifying frame is:

- Texas as a natural laboratory for sustainability research
- two co-led pilot projects rather than four disconnected mini-agendas
- shared doctoral training, shared data infrastructure, and shared grant development

Use the name **Texas Sustainability Research Collaborative (TSRC)** if the group likes it. If they do not, keep the logic and drop the branding.

## Recommended Presentation Shape

Target: **6 slides, 18 minutes total**

| Slide | Purpose | Lead speakers | Time |
|------|---------|---------------|------|
| 1 | Framing: spire of excellence + Texas thesis | Andrews | 2 min |
| 2 | Pilot A: equitable environmental infrastructure + community resilience | Bray + Marable | 4 min |
| 3 | Pilot B: land use, conservation, climate-resilient development | Andrews + Shost | 3 min |
| 4 | PhD pipeline + pedagogy + student integration | Bray + Marable | 3 min |
| 5 | Funding strategy: federal + Texas/state/local pipeline | Andrews + Bray | 3 min |
| 6 | 18-month roadmap + governance + close | Shost | 3 min |

**Important simplification:** do not use a separate "researcher spotlight" slide unless the group strongly insists. Fold each person's contribution into the pilot and funding slides. That keeps the talk tighter and more collaborative.

## Slide-by-Slide Build Notes

### Slide 1: Framing

**Message:** A spire of excellence is shared infrastructure, shared students, shared grants, and a shared place-based research agenda.

Must include:

- Texas and DFW as the proving ground
- the four pillars from the prompt
- the claim that the cluster will operate as one system

Visual:

- simple Texas map or four-pillar diagram

Avoid:

- long self-introductions
- abstract language with no place anchor

### Slide 2: Pilot A

**Message:** The first pilot addresses equitable environmental infrastructure and community resilience.

Must include:

- water, mobility, and hazard-management systems
- equity and community resilience outcomes
- one or two likely partners: City of Arlington, TRWD, NCTCOG, TDEM
- a concrete student output such as a policy memo or field-based project

### Slide 3: Pilot B

**Message:** The second pilot addresses land use, conservation, and climate-resilient development.

Must include:

- parcel/land-use/floodplain data
- conservation finance, affordability, and planning tradeoffs
- one clear policy or design question
- Andrews/Shost complementarity: spatial analysis plus economics/policy logic

### Slide 4: PhD Pipeline + Pedagogy

**Message:** The cluster is not only a grant machine; it is a student-training model.

Must include:

- cross-advising model
- annual sustainability methods seminar
- dissertation studio
- Year 1 target of 4 doctoral students, growing to 8 at steady state
- undergraduate practicum or capstone pathway

Avoid:

- listing too many courses
- turning this into a catalog instead of a model

### Slide 5: Funding Strategy

**Message:** The cluster has a near-term funding plan, a flagship aspiration grant, and Texas-specific seed pathways.

Must include:

- **Lead with NSF DRMS** as the first concrete Year 1 move
- position **NSF GCR** as the flagship convergence target
- name one agriculture/resource pathway: USDA AFRI
- name one climate/resilience pathway: NOAA or NSF S&CC
- weave in **Meadows Foundation** and **NCTCOG NTCMT**
- mention one Texas agency example: TWDB, GLO, TxDOT, or TCEQ

Do not:

- recite every grant in the memo
- over-claim uncertain programs

### Slide 6: Roadmap + Governance + Close

**Message:** The cluster can act immediately and has a structure to sustain the work.

Must include:

- Fall 2026: first proposals, shared meetings, partner outreach, initial student recruitment
- Spring/Fall 2027: methods seminar, fieldwork, GCR build, first co-authored output
- governance: biweekly meetings, rotating coordination, shared data/workspace
- close on why this makes UTA visibly distinctive

## First 10 Minutes in the Room

Use this sequence.

### Minute 0-2: Fast introductions

Each person gives:

- name
- field
- one sentence on what they bring to the cluster

### Minute 2-4: Set the task

Suggested language:

> We should optimize for a structure that answers the whole prompt, gives each of us a clear role, and shows a real collaborative model rather than four adjacent research agendas.

### Minute 4-6: Offer the six-slide structure

Suggested language:

> A simple way to do that is: one shared frame, two co-led pilot projects, one student-training slide, one funding slide, and one rollout slide.

### Minute 6-8: Invite revision without losing control

Suggested language:

> If that structure works, we can adjust the wording and emphasis together, but it gives us something concrete to build in the next hour.

### Minute 8-10: Lock roles

Decide immediately:

- who edits slides
- who timekeeps
- who owns each slide
- who handles the final transition rehearsal

## Suggested 2-Hour Build Agenda

| Time block | Goal | Output |
|-----------|------|--------|
| 0-15 min | agree on thesis, slide order, owners | shared structure locked |
| 15-40 min | build Slides 2-3 first | both pilot slides drafted |
| 40-60 min | build Slide 4 | PhD/pedagogy model drafted |
| 60-85 min | build Slide 5 | funding slide with 5-6 named targets |
| 85-100 min | build Slides 1 and 6 | framing and close drafted |
| 100-115 min | simplify language and visuals | clean final deck |
| 115-120 min | full run-through | timing and handoffs confirmed |

**Why this order:** the pilots and funding logic are the hardest pieces to align across disciplines. Build those first.

## Default Role Map

Use this only as a starting point.

| Role | Default owner | Why |
|------|---------------|-----|
| Facilitator / opener | Andrews | already has the strongest full-plan synthesis |
| Equity / community voice | Bray | strongest fit for community-engaged framing |
| Infrastructure / policy economics | Marable | strong fit for mobility, infrastructure, applied economics |
| Economics / conservation / close | Shost | strong fit for policy rigor and concise synthesis |
| Slide editor | whoever is fastest in PowerPoint or Google Slides | speed matters more than ownership |
| Timekeeper | non-editor | avoids split attention |

## What to Push Hard

- one shared thesis
- the two-pilot structure
- the student-training model as a real differentiator
- a funding pipeline with immediate and medium-term targets
- Texas-specific credibility

## What to Stay Flexible On

- the TSRC name
- exact pilot labels
- which person speaks first after the opener
- how much emphasis goes to pedagogy versus governance

## What to Cut First if Time Shrinks

If the committee compresses the talk, cut in this order:

1. standalone researcher spotlights
2. detailed governance language
3. long course lists
4. lower-probability grant examples

Keep at all costs:

- shared framing
- two pilot projects
- student pipeline
- one near-term grant and one flagship grant
- 12-18 month action plan

## Backup 5-Slide Version

If needed, merge the material like this:

1. Framing + Texas thesis
2. Pilot A
3. Pilot B
4. Students + pedagogy + funding
5. Roadmap + governance + close
