## Tier 1: Active and Actionable

### Meadows Foundation — Environment Program
_Dallas-based, one of Texas's largest private foundations_

- **Status:** ACTIVE — accepting proposals year-round
- **Funding:** Documented grants to Texas universities range from $500K (Texas State for climate change research) to $2.75M (Sul Ross for conservation) to $4M (UT Austin). Total environment giving: $76M cumulative
- **Focus:** Preserving ecological and biological resources in the context of Texas population growth
- **Eligibility:** 501(c)(3) public charities or public entities serving Texas. Universities qualify
- **Application:** Online portal; one proposal per 12 months; 4–5 month review cycle; board meets Jan, Apr, Jun, Sep, Nov
- **URL:** mfi.org/how-to-apply/

**Cluster fit:** This is the strongest philanthropic match for the cluster. A proposal on "Building Texas Sustainability Research Infrastructure at UTA" — framing the shared data platform, PhD pipeline, and community partnerships — could secure significant seed funding that makes every federal proposal stronger.

**Role map:**
- Andrews (PI): Spatial data infrastructure, flood research, community GIS
- Bray (co-PI): Environmental justice, community engagement
- Shost (co-PI): Conservation economics, land use
- Marable (co-PI): Water quality, infrastructure economics

**Strategy:** Submit a Meadows proposal in Fall 2026 as the cluster's first non-federal application. Frame it as capacity-building for the Texas Sustainability Research Collaborative.

### NCTCOG North Texas Center for Mobility Technologies (NTCMT)

- **Status:** ACTIVE regional pathway — public materials confirm the center and university participation, but current application mechanics should be confirmed directly with NCTCOG
- **Funding:** Public NCTCOG materials support a mobility-research partnership structure. Confirm current matching levels and cost-share terms directly with NCTCOG before citing specifics
- **Eligibility:** UTA is already part of the regional mobility research ecosystem reflected in NCTCOG materials
- **Focus:** Transportation/mobility technology: EVs, autonomous vehicles, fuel cells, micro-mobility, freight, multimodal transit

**Cluster fit:** Directly relevant to Marable's EV infrastructure research and Andrews' spatial analysis of transportation systems. This is one of the clearest regional pathways tying the cluster to North Texas mobility work.

**Role map:**
- Marable (PI): EV charging infrastructure, consumer behavior, transportation economics
- Andrews (co-PI): Spatial analysis of EV network coverage, accessibility modeling

### Texas General Land Office — Coastal Management Program (Cycle 32)

- **Status:** ACTIVE — applications opened March 1, 2026
- **Deadline:** **June 3, 2026, 5:00 PM** (imminent)
- **Funding:** NOAA-funded and GOMESA-funded projects for coastal habitat protection, hazard mitigation, flood data modeling, living shorelines, resilience planning
- **Workshops:** In-person and virtual; registration closes March 20, 2026
- **URL:** glo.texas.gov/coastal/protecting-coast/coastal-management-program

**Cluster fit:** Strong match for Andrews' flood risk research and Bray's climate adaptation / environmental justice work along the Texas Gulf Coast.

**Role map:**
- Andrews (PI): Flood data modeling, spatial analysis, coastal hazard mapping
- Bray (co-PI): Community resilience, environmental justice in coastal communities

**Strategy:** This is a near-term opportunity — deadline is June 3. Mention in the Day-1 presentation as an example of state-level funding the cluster would pursue immediately.

### TWDB Flood Priority Research Program

- **Status:** ACTIVE — biennial program, ~$600–700K per biennium across 4–5 projects
- **Topics:** Flood education, early warning systems, infrastructure assessment, nature-based mitigation, rainfall frequency projections, agricultural flood loss modeling
- **Eligibility:** Through contractor relationships; past collaborators include UT Austin, Texas A&M, engineering firms. UTA's Water Engineering Research Center (WERC) has received TWDB grants
- **Contact:** floodpriorityresearch@twdb.texas.gov

**Cluster fit:** Directly aligned with Andrews' flood risk / spatial analysis research. UTA already has a relationship through WERC.

**Strategy:** Contact TWDB in Fall 2026 to position for the next biennial cycle. Propose a project linking flood risk modeling (Andrews) with economic impact analysis (Marable/Shost) and community resilience (Bray).

---

### TCEQ Section 319(h) Nonpoint Source Grants

- **Status:** ACTIVE — periodic solicitations
- **Funding:** 60% federal reimbursement / 40% applicant match (in-kind allowed)
- **Focus:** Watershed-based plans addressing nonpoint source water pollution; water quality research
- **Eligibility:** State universities eligible as direct applicants
- **Contact:** nps@tceq.texas.gov

**Cluster fit:** Water quality research linking Marable's consumer response to water violations, Andrews' spatial analysis of watershed pollution sources, and Bray's environmental justice assessment of who is exposed.

## Tier 2: Prepare for Next Cycle

### TxDOT Research and Technology Implementation (RTI) Program

- **Status:** ACTIVE — annual cycle. FY2026 proposals were due March 5, 2026 (just passed)
- **Funding:** ~80% federal SPR funds / 20% state match
- **Eligibility:** Texas state-supported institutions of higher education only (UTA eligible)
- **Process:** TxDOT issues Problem Statements → converted to Project Statements → RFP → competitive university proposals
- **Next cycle:** FY2027 call likely late 2026 / early 2027

**Cluster fit:** Transportation infrastructure, EV infrastructure, flood-resilient transportation systems. Natural fit for Marable and Andrews.

**Strategy:** Monitor TxDOT Problem Statements when FY2027 call opens. Propose projects on EV infrastructure equity or flood impacts on transportation networks.

### UTA Interdisciplinary Research Program (IRP)

- **Status:** ACTIVE — annual cycle. FY2026-27 applications closed December 2025; next cycle ~November 2026
- **Funding:** ~$20K per grant; UTA distributed $150K+ in 2025
- **Alignment:** IRP explicitly supports research in **Sustainable Communities** and **Global Environmental Impact** — directly matching the cluster's themes
- **Requirement:** Must submit at least one external interdisciplinary proposal within one year of receiving IRP funding

**Cluster fit:** Perfect seed grant for a pilot project that generates preliminary data for a larger NSF or USDA proposal. All four candidates apply jointly.

**Strategy:** Submit IRP application in November 2026 for a pilot data collection project in the DFW study region. Use the IRP requirement to submit an external proposal as motivation for the DRMS or AFRI application.

### UT System STARs / Rising STARs Program

- **Status:** ACTIVE — institutional process through UTA Provost/VP for Research
- **Funding:** Up to $500K per STARs faculty recruit; up to $300K per Rising STARs recruit
- **Purpose:** Equipment purchases and lab renovations for newly recruited research faculty
- **Eligibility:** UT System institutions recruiting new research faculty

**Cluster fit:** Each cluster hire may be eligible for STARs/Rising STARs funding to set up research infrastructure. This is negotiated during the hiring process, not applied for separately — but it's worth mentioning in the presentation as evidence that UTA invests in new faculty capacity.

## Tier 3: Institutional and Network

### Governor's University Research Initiative (GURI)

- **Status:** ACTIVE — rolling applications
- **Funding:** Matching grants up to **$5M per distinguished researcher**
- **Eligibility:** Texas public universities recruiting National Academy members, Nobel Laureates, or equivalent
- **Application:** Through governor's office portal

**Cluster fit:** Not applicable to current cluster hires (requires NAS-level distinction), but worth noting as a UTA resource. If the cluster recruits a senior sustainability leader in Years 3–5, GURI becomes relevant.

---

### NCTCOG RISE Coalition (Regional Integration of Sustainability Efforts)

- **Status:** ACTIVE — ongoing membership program
- **What it provides:** Technical assistance, training, resources for sustainability initiatives. Not a grant, but a network
- **Eligibility:** Universities eligible as members

**Cluster fit:** Join immediately. Provides regional visibility, connections to local government sustainability offices, and collaborative project opportunities. Low cost, high networking value.

## How to Present This in the Day-1 Exercise

State and local funding should be woven into the main funding strategy, not presented as a separate section. Key talking points:

**When discussing Year 1 seed grants:**
> "Beyond federal funding, we'll immediately pursue the **Meadows Foundation** — one of Texas's largest private foundations with a strong environment program and a track record of $500K–$2.75M grants to Texas universities for exactly this kind of work."

**When discussing community partnerships:**
> "UTA is already positioned in the North Texas mobility ecosystem through **NCTCOG's North Texas Center for Mobility Technologies**, which gives the cluster a real regional pathway for transportation and EV research. We'll also join the **NCTCOG RISE Coalition** to embed the cluster in the regional sustainability network."

**When discussing flood/resilience research:**
> "The **Texas Water Development Board** funds university flood research directly — UTA's Water Engineering Research Center already has TWDB grants. We'll position for the next biennial cycle with a proposal linking spatial flood modeling, economic impact analysis, and community resilience."

**When discussing the Texas advantage:**
> "Texas has its own funding ecosystem for sustainability research — from the **GLO Coastal Management Program** (applications open now, due June 3) to **TxDOT's annual university research program** to the **TCEQ Section 319(h)** water quality grants. These state funds provide seed data and community partnerships that make our federal proposals more competitive."

## Strategic Notes

1. **Meadows Foundation is the top priority.** It's the closest thing to a sure bet — a major Texas funder with proven interest in university environmental research, rolling applications, and no match requirement. A well-crafted proposal could secure $500K–$1M in flexible seed funding.

2. **NCTCOG NTCMT is a high-potential regional pathway, not a throwaway line.** UTA is already connected to that ecosystem, and Marable's EV research fits well. Confirm the current cycle and matching terms early, then decide whether it should become one of the first regional proposals.

3. **State agencies build the Texas story.** Mentioning TWDB, GLO, TCEQ, and TxDOT in the presentation demonstrates that the cluster understands Texas's unique funding landscape — not just federal agencies. This signals local expertise.

4. **Regional partners are co-PI magnets.** TRWD, TRA, and City of Arlington aren't funders, but they're partners that make proposals to NOAA, FEMA, EPA, and NSF much stronger. Establish these relationships in Semester 1.

5. **UTA's IRP is the cheapest pilot grant.** ~$20K won't fund much, but it comes with an institutional expectation to submit an external proposal — which is exactly what the cluster needs to do anyway.

6. **GLO CMP Cycle 32 is the nearest state deadline (June 3).** If the cluster is hired and onboarded quickly, this could be a first-semester win.
